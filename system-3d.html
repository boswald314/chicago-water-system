Scale

Horizontal distances are always true — nothing here stretches them. The system is about 50 km across and 90 m deep, so at 1:1 it reads as a sheet, and an 8-metre tunnel is a hair. The vertical slider stretches the whole vertical axis, the standard geologic-section convention: depths, tank heights and reservoir depths all grow together, so shapes are honest in plan but taller than life. The conduit slider widens pipe and shaft radii only, and never touches reservoir, tank or building dimensions. Set both to 1:1, or use the “fly here at true 1:1 scale” button on any inspected object, to see true shape.

The simulation

A mass balance, not a hydraulic model. It conserves volume across the published storage capacities, plant design flows and pump capacities, and it routes each basin's excess down the tunnel system that actually serves it. It does not solve for head, velocity, air entrainment, surge or travel time, so it will not tell you when a particular shaft chokes or where a pressure wave goes. Its one fitted parameter is the runoff coefficient, set to 0.32 because that is the value that reproduces MWRD's recorded pumping-station discharge for 13 September 2008.
